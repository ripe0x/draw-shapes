This is substantially better. I would let the agent proceed with the market proof, but with the corrections below built into its acceptance tests. I would not yet approve the larger game refactor.

The revision fixes most of the earlier structural problems: it has recovered the existing branches, restored the original sponsor terms, preserved operating-capital protection and retirement, separated scheduled tokens from purchased rewards, and put the market proof ahead of the refactor. Those are the right changes.

The remaining problems are specific implementation errors and incomplete guarantees. They do not require reopening the overall product design. I checked the relevant Uniswap documentation and the repository's FWA implementation. Nothing below should be mistaken for a compiled contract test.

## 1. The seven-day limit does not actually prevent an epoch from getting stuck

This is the most important new finding.

The revised plan requires every request in an epoch to leave Pending, Ready, or DeliveryPending before finalizing. It says FWA's seven-day delivery window bounds that wait.

The FWA implementation does something more specific:

Before the deadline: anyone can retry delivery.

After the deadline: only the purchaser can request the failed-delivery refund.

The status does not automatically change when seven days pass. `retryNFTDelivery()` becomes unavailable, while `refundFailedDelivery()` still requires the purchaser to call it.

Therefore, one failed delivery whose purchaser never requests the refund can leave an entire epoch unclaimable indefinitely under the proposed finalization rule.

### Required fix

Separate resolution for genesis rewards from completion of the purchaser's FWA refund.

Once a failed delivery's retry deadline has elapsed, Genesis can classify that request as ineligible for rewards without waiting for the purchaser to withdraw or request their refund. That must not interfere with their FWA refund rights.

Also, before finalizing the epoch, reconcile every request that became Fulfilled after the ordinary booking walk passed it. Checking that the status is now terminal is not enough if `creditLate()` has not yet been called.

The acceptance test should be:
One request fails delivery. Its purchaser never takes further action. All other successful purchasers can still claim their correct epoch rewards after the defined deadline.

Use bounded processing and a resolution cursor or counters. Do not solve this by scanning an unbounded day's worth of requests in one transaction.

## 2. The primary purchase and ETH-only liquidity sequence still does not work as written

The plan now correctly commits primary purchase proceeds to market liquidity. That fixes the missing destination.

But its actual sequence is:

Buy original tokens from the separate curve → calculate the higher ending curve price → add an ETH-only position ending at that higher price.

The problem is that a purchase from the separate curve does not itself move the Uniswap pool's price.

For example, using the proposed curve and a first purchase of 0.05 ETH:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Initial curve and pool price | 0.000000050000 ETH per ENGINE |
| Curve price after the purchase | Approximately 0.000000050128 |
| Pool price after that separate curve purchase | Still 0.000000050000 |
| Proposed new position | From one tenth of the new curve price to the new curve price |

The actual pool price falls inside the proposed position.

A position containing the current price requires both assets, rather than ETH alone. Uniswap's liquidity documentation explicitly distinguishes positions inside the current price range from single-sided positions at or beyond a boundary.

### Required fix

Write the complete executable sequence for moving the pool, purchasing original tokens, and installing the funded bids. The liquidity range must be valid against the actual pool state when it is added.

Do not silently take extra ENGINE from original inventory to make a supposedly ETH-only deposit work.

Also define the price orientation correctly. With native ETH as currency0 and ENGINE as currency1, Uniswap's square-root price represents ENGINE per ETH, whereas this plan defines pCurve as ETH per ENGINE. The conversion to `sqrtPriceLimitX96` needs the reciprocal, decimal normalization, and Q96 scaling.

"The immediate sale test passes by construction" is premature. That test is precisely what must establish whether the proposed sequence works. The plan currently makes that claim before implementing it.

### Correct the proceeds invariant too

I17 says primary proceeds received equal ETH principal in bid positions plus pending deployment. But after a farmer sells, some of that ETH has left the position and the position holds ENGINE instead.

Keep two separate reconciliations:

Historical commitment: primary proceeds received equal primary proceeds committed to liquidity plus any pending amount.

Current assets: reconcile the actual ETH and ENGINE remaining in the positions after trades.

Otherwise the invariant either becomes false after the first sale or merely tracks historical deposits while appearing to prove current ETH backing.

## 3. Two curve calculations need correction before implementation

### The exponential curve is not calibrated to the quoted liquidity curve

The plan specifies `p(q) = p0 × 10^(q / Q)` with `p0 = 5e-8` ETH and `Q = 900 million` ENGINE. It then says the curve is calibrated to the approximately 142 ETH required by the original concentrated-liquidity position.

Integrating the stated formula gives `p0 × Q × (10 − 1) / ln 10 ≈ 175.89 ETH`.

The corresponding single concentrated-liquidity position with those endpoints requires approximately 142.30 ETH to buy all its original tokens.

That is a 23.6% difference. Matching opening price, ending price, and token quantity does not make the curves economically equivalent.

My recommendation is straightforward: treat the exponential curve as a changed design and rerun it honestly. Do not preserve the claim that it already matches the earlier model.

### The reward purchase's minOut is too high

The plan proposes `minOut = budget / pCurve` and calls that a floor on output.

For an increasing price curve, dividing by the starting price overestimates how many tokens the budget purchases.

Using the same first 0.05 ETH purchase:

| Calculation | ENGINE |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed budget / pCurve minimum | 1,000,000 |
| Actual output from the stated curve | Approximately 998,723 |

A valid primary purchase would revert if implemented with that minimum.

Use the exact curve quote, conservative rounding, and the intended execution tolerance. For combined secondary and primary purchases, calculate the bound for the actual route, not one starting spot price.

These are arithmetic corrections, not choices that need another owner decision.

## 4. The transfer authorization is cleared before ordinary settlement needs it

The revised transfer gate is much better than the original wallet allowlist. However, it now says authorization is set in `beforeSwap` and cleared in `afterSwap`.

In v4, `afterSwap` is not the end of settlement. PoolManager calls it before returning the swap's deltas; the router can then transfer the input tokens and take the output tokens afterward. I checked that ordering in PoolManager's source.

If authorization disappears in `afterSwap`, a normal router can find that its subsequent ENGINE transfer is prohibited.

### Required fix

Authorization must remain available through the intended settlement path and be consumed against the actual permitted transfers. Test the entire unlock and settlement transaction, including multiple swaps and net settlement, rather than only the callbacks.

There is a second issue: ERC-6909 does not necessarily invoke an ENGINE ERC-20 transfer at all.

Uniswap expressly supports resolving balances by minting and burning internal claims instead of transferring the underlying token. Therefore, the plan's assertion that those paths all require an ENGINE transfer is incorrect.

This does not automatically expose original inventory held outside the pool. It does mean that the transfer gate alone does not prove the claimed restrictions on alternate trading or claim transfers.

Require an explicit test using ERC-6909 credits, net settlement, and another pool. Either demonstrate the intended restriction or narrow the promise to what is actually enforceable.

Finally, distinguish an alternate router using the authorized canonical pool from an attempt to bypass the canonical market. The test list currently says a second router and direct unlock should fail wholesale. That is broader than necessary and risks breaking ordinary integrations.

## 5. Claiming earned farming rewards should not depend on a successful market purchase

The plan runs `buyForRewards()` inside `finaliseEpoch()`. After genesis, it proposes executing against a 30-minute pool TWAP, leaving an unfillable purchase tagged for retry.

Two problems remain.

First, a failed swap can prevent finalization and therefore prevent claims of the separately funded scheduled allocation.

Farmers should not lose access to their already funded scheduled ENGINE because a purchase of additional rewards cannot execute.

Second, Uniswap v4 does not include a built-in TWAP oracle. "The pool's 30-minute TWAP" is a new dependency that has not been designed here. An observation mechanism must be implemented, initialized, maintained, and protected against insufficient liquidity or history.

### My decision

Separate these operations:

Finalize successful draw counts, bonus weights, and the scheduled reward allocation.

Execute the epoch's purchased-reward budget independently, then credit the same finalized cohort through a separate accounting index or tranche.

That preserves source-epoch attribution without allowing market execution to hold all claims hostage.

The plan must also explain what happens when retained earnings attributable to an epoch become distributable after its first finalization. A single purchase inside finalization does not settle that case.

Do not add an implicit oracle merely to make the pseudocode look complete. Choose and test an actual execution protection, and keep its failure isolated from already earned claims.

## 6. Three smaller but important accounting and lifecycle fixes

| Issue | Required correction |
| --- | --- |
| The operating floor subtracts mint fees twice | The opening 1.122 ETH already equals 4 − 2.730 − 0.148. Keep the floor at 1.122 ETH unless deliberately changing the reserve policy. Do not subtract the 0.148 ETH again. |
| Pack terms are fixed at both mint and request acceptance | Choose one authoritative rule. My preference is to snapshot haircut and delay on the accepted request, then bind those terms to the selected pack when the award is verified. |
| The delivery timestamp is assumed to be readable afterward | Record a verifiable timestamp through the wrapper's actual delivery path, or explicitly use another available timestamp. Do not assume FWA exposes a historical delivery timestamp. |

The floor wording appears in the revised game ledger. The conflicting pack timing appears in D3, the architecture table, and the proposed mint interface.

For the timestamp, the inspected FWA getters expose request block, listing, status, and pending-delivery deadline. The successful delivery implementation deletes the pending-delivery record and does not store the historical timestamp assumed by `registerAward()`.

I would also add one explicit deadline test:
Genesis has ended, nobody has called `finalise()`, and a winner attempts an authenticated purchase.

That purchase must not release original tokens. Original issuance must stop because of the deadline itself, not because a keeper eventually burns the inventory. The current finalizer section describes the burn but the primary purchase steps do not explicitly state this time guard.

## What I would approve now

Approve the revised product direction, branch reuse, and market-first implementation order. The plan correctly makes the new market prove itself before the large refactor and acknowledges that the earlier model does not validate the replacement architecture.

I would make the following results mandatory before Phase 3:

| Proof | What must be demonstrated |
| --- | --- |
| Primary purchase and immediate sale | The exact original-token release, liquidity placement, sale proceeds, and remaining assets reconcile |
| Public trading | Normal supported routers work; unauthorized original distribution does not |
| Fee and transfer handling | All four swap forms, partial fills, ordinary settlement, and ERC-6909 behavior are covered |
| Genesis closure | Original issuance stops on time even without a finalizer call |
| Reward availability | Failed delivery or failed reward purchases cannot indefinitely block unrelated earned claims |

No further tokenomics exploration is needed to address these points. They are tests and corrections to the chosen mechanism.

One useful instruction to add to the plan: if the market proof fails, the coding agent should compare the already specified fallback using the same harness and document its recommendation. It should not begin refactoring the entire game around an unproven market merely to maintain momentum.

## Bottom line

Revision 2 is a meaningful improvement, and the project should move forward into the bounded market proof.

The two largest remaining risks are:

The primary purchase proceeds are committed to a liquidity placement that is not actually ETH-only at the stated pool price.

Epoch claims can remain blocked indefinitely because FWA's seven-day window does not automatically resolve failed deliveries.

Fix those, correct the curve and minOut arithmetic, preserve transfer authorization through settlement, and separate scheduled claims from reward-purchase execution.

I would send those corrections back as required implementation tests, not ask for another broad redesign.
