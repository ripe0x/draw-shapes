## 2. The proposed market architecture has an important missing ETH flow

**Holding undistributed tokens outside the LP is a promising simplification. But the plan has not yet specified the economically equivalent market.**

Under A3, the hook holds original tokens and sells them to authenticated game buyers through a virtual curve. Public participants trade against the separate v4 pool.

The missing question is:

**When an ENGINE Pack spends 0.05 ETH purchasing original tokens from the hook, exactly where does that 0.05 ETH go?**

Our original mechanism depended on that payment becoming liquidity against which holders could sell. In the new architecture, paying a separate issuance curve does not automatically put ETH into the public pool.

If the proceeds stay elsewhere while farmers sell into the original 0.25 ETH bid position, this design recreates the liquidity problem we were trying to avoid.

### Required correction

Specify a separate, conserved **primary purchase proceeds account** and the mechanism that commits its ETH to selling liquidity or an equivalent funded bid facility.

That ETH must not be treated as game profit, trading fees, or money available for the GRAIL and Punk funds.

The first acceptance test should be:

> An ENGINE Pack buys original tokens. The holder immediately sells them. Show the exact ETH and token balances before and after, including where the primary purchase ETH supported that sale.

### "Add full range liquidity" is also incomplete

The plan says the liquidity allocation will be added as full range positions whenever fees are allocated. But it identifies an ETH fee stream without specifying where the necessary circulated ENGINE comes from. A full range position at an interior price requires both assets. [Uniswap Developers](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/flash-accounting)

Using untouched original inventory would violate the distribution rule. Buying circulated tokens would be an additional market transaction that must be funded and modeled. An ETH bid position is another possibility, but behaves differently.

**Choose and model the actual policy. Do not leave "liquidity" as an accounting label without the asset movements behind it.**

### The routing rule is not yet precise enough

The review recommends selling original tokens at no less than pool spot, while also suggesting routing to the original curve when the pool is more expensive. That needs a complete definition, not a one-line price comparison.

Execution depends on trade size, available liquidity, and movement through both curves. A current spot quote alone does not establish an acceptable execution price. Uniswap specifically identifies manipulated prices and inconsistencies between custom curves as hook risks. [Uniswap Developers](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/security)

**My decision:** accept separate custody of original tokens as a candidate implementation improvement. Do not carry forward the old model's liquidity and price results until this exact replacement architecture reproduces or improves them.

## 3. The proposed transfer gate blocks the users it is meant to serve

The plan says that, during genesis, transfers require **both sender and recipient** to be in a fixed allowlist containing the protocol contracts and PoolManager.

Taken literally, that blocks:

| Intended action | Why the proposed rule rejects it |
|---|---|
| Farmer claims rewards | Farmer's wallet is not on the allowlist |
| ENGINE Pack delivers purchased coins | Recipient wallet is not on the allowlist |
| Farmer sells coins | Farmer's wallet is not on the allowlist |
| Public buyer receives circulated coins | Buyer's wallet is not on the allowlist |

Changing "both" to "either" is not a sufficient fix either. Blanket trust in PoolManager transfers could allow paths unrelated to the authorized ENGINE market.

**Required correction:** use narrowly authenticated transfer permissions for distribution, pack settlement, and actual canonical pool swaps. Bind market permissions to the correct pool, direction, amount, and transaction.

Also define prelaunch behavior explicitly. A zero or unset genesis deadline must not accidentally make transfers unrestricted before launch.

This needs an executable demonstration early, before the rest of the system is built around the gate.

## 4. The haircut improves the ETH exit, but does not establish ENGINE conversion demand

The review was right to challenge my original unconditional full-backing exit. I should have put more weight on a farmer's ability to wait and take cash instead of buying and immediately selling ENGINE.

However, **a 5% cash-exit haircut does not generally make the two routes economically identical**.

The plan changes a 0.05 ETH ENGINE Pack into either:

**An immediate 0.05 ETH ENGINE purchase**, or

**A 0.0475 ETH cash exit after 48 hours.**

Even with the same nominal sell fee, the market route can involve different execution prices, limited bid liquidity, and additional transaction costs. This matters particularly when buying from the primary curve and selling into a separate pool.

Therefore, the haircut can reduce the preference for cash, but **it does not justify treating 40% of outcomes as dependable market purchases**.

### My recommendation

Keep the haircut option, but make three requirements explicit:

**First:** model zero, partial, and full conversion. A functioning baseline game must not depend on every ENGINE Pack being converted.

**Second:** snapshot the exit haircut and applicable rights when the draw is accepted. A timelocked parameter change should affect future prizes, not worsen an existing winner's claim.

**Third:** report ETH-exit fees separately from external market trading fees. A fee taken from prize backing is not new speculative demand.

The review's request to disclose gross purchases alongside net market ETH retention is also correct. Adopt it.

## 5. The revised Punk offer is not economically "fair" as described

**I would reject the planner's claim that paying `P / 100,000` with a `1.0 P` cap is a fair replacement for the previous offer.**

It considers the chance of losing the Punk during individual draws, but misses the consequence of reaching the cap: **the sponsor also relinquishes the Punk when fully compensated**.

Here is a calculation using the plan's terms, with explicit assumptions:

**Reference value:** 30 ETH.
**Win probability:** exactly 1 in 100,000 per eligible draw.
**Compensation:** 0.0003 ETH per draw.
**Cap:** 30 ETH, reached after 100,000 draws.
**Assume:** continuous funded play, no reclaim, and payment includes the winning draw.

If the Punk is won early, payments stop. If it survives to the cap, the game keeps it. Either way, the sponsor eventually gives up the NFT.

Expected payments are:

30 × (1 − (1 − 0.00001)^100,000) ≈ **18.96 ETH**.

That is roughly **19 ETH of expected compensation for surrendering a 30 ETH asset**, before considering payment delay.

For comparison, under the same simplified assumptions, the earlier **2× exposure rate with a 1.5× cap** produces approximately **31.66 ETH of expected compensation**. That does not prove the earlier offer is attractive enough, but it shows why calling it "twice the fair rate" is misleading.

The 30-day reclaim right changes the outcome because the sponsor can recover the unwon NFT. At the proposed 1× exposure rate, it can approximately compensate expected loss during a limited commitment at the stated maximum odds, **without providing a risk premium**. It also makes reclaim more attractive than remaining indefinitely until the below-cost acquisition completes.

### My recommendation

**Do not implement the new rate and cap as settled economics.** Restore the distinction between:

**Compensation for temporary prize exposure**, and

**Compensation for permanently transferring ownership.**

Use the earlier 2× rate and 1.5× cap as a comparison case rather than automatically replacing it with 1×/1×. Keep the sponsor optional and funded only from its stated budget.

The planner is right that sponsorship uptime depends on the pot. That should be disclosed and tested. It is not a reason to label an unattractive offer fair.

## 6. Remove depositor shares, but preserve capital protection and shutdown

The plan removes NAV, reserve parameters, and all exit and export functionality. It introduces a retained-surplus bucket that is swept to Treasury after positive margin is split.

**Removing public ownership accounting is appropriate. Removing the ability to measure and protect operating capital is not.**

The implementation still needs to establish:

**Which assets belong to the game.**

**Which assets are already committed to winners or refunds.**

**How much capital is required for the next replenishment and maintenance operations.**

**Whether earlier losses have been recovered before money leaves for rewards or burns.**

The current accounting carries negative margin forward and keeps the retained allocation inside ordinary assets. The proposed sweep changes that behavior materially.

A sequence of cheap outcomes can create apparent distributable surplus, followed by an expensive outcome that consumes operating cash. A price markup provides an expected edge; it does not make every draw profitable.

**Required correction:** define an explicit distribution gate based on realized losses, available cash, replacement obligations, and required operating coverage. Test adverse outcome orderings, not only average return.

Likewise, keep a minimal retirement process:

Stop new draws, settle existing requests, recover unawarded inventory, return sponsor property where applicable, and dispose of residual assets according to a published rule.

Removing depositor withdrawal queues does not justify deleting the game's emergency and terminal lifecycle.

## 7. The epoch accounting needs to distinguish scheduled issuance from recycled rewards

The plan defines an invariant that all farming tokens claimed, claimable, reserved, and burned sum to **100 million**. It also adds market-purchased tokens to the same epoch reward pots. Those two statements do not reconcile without separate accounting.

**Required correction:** track scheduled original farming tokens separately from tokens acquired through game-funded purchases.

Both can be distributed through the same claim interface. Their supply provenance and conservation equations must remain distinct.

There is also a timing issue: the plan redirects purchases made after an epoch finalizes into the next epoch, then burns them after the final epoch. That gives transaction timing an influence over which farmers benefit.

Define the attribution rule explicitly. Prefer assigning eligible earnings or purchase budgets to their economic source epoch, rather than letting an arbitrary keeper delay determine the beneficiary cohort.

### Delivery states need a complete rule

The review proposes no reward for a request written off while delivery is pending, even if delivery later completes. Meanwhile, the pack's award timestamp is recorded only during the booked successful-delivery walk.

That leaves an important case:

**FWA eventually delivers the pack, but the local accounting has already passed that request.**

Specify how that winner obtains the pack's redemption rights and whether they earn their genesis allocation. A conservative accounting write-off should not accidentally become permanent loss of the purchaser's rights.

The test matrix should include delivery before bookkeeping, delayed successful delivery, refund, transfer before redemption, and settlement after genesis closes.

## 8. Several v4 and permission details need correction

These are implementation requirements, not reasons to abandon the custom market.

### ETH-side fees cannot be specified as "charge in afterSwap" for every swap

The plan proposes charging the ETH-side fee in `afterSwap`, but does not list `afterSwapReturnDelta` among its permissions.

Uniswap's return-delta rules distinguish specified and unspecified currencies. An `afterSwap` return delta affects the unspecified currency, which is not ETH for every buy/sell and exact-input/exact-output combination. [Uniswap Developers](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/custom-accounting)

Require a fee matrix covering all four swap forms, partial fills, refunds, and exact treasury receipts. Select the hook permissions from that implementation rather than discovering after deployment that a required callback permission is missing.

### Do not add liquidity on every fee allocation inside the swap

The plan forwards fees to Treasury during the swap and proposes adding full range liquidity on each allocation.

That creates unnecessary coupling between ordinary trading, treasury routing, and liquidity management.

**My recommendation:** collect and account for fees during the swap, then perform bounded allocation and liquidity deployment separately. v4 requires all transaction deltas to settle, and calls made inside hook execution require careful ordering and reentrancy analysis. [Uniswap Developers](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/guides/flash-accounting)

### "Keeper tunables" are not automatically harmless

The plan leaves tips, batching, and timing parameters directly changeable by the multisig. Those can alter operating expenditure and availability.

Allow immediate risk reduction, but impose hard bounds or timelocks on changes that increase spending or weaken safeguards. In particular, a privileged tip increase must not provide an indirect way to consume the maintenance budget.

### Sponsor reservation must cover the whole batch

The plan says to reserve compensation for "pending + 1 draws," although the game supports batch purchases.

Reserve for **every newly accepted draw in the batch**, plus existing exposure. Exhaustion must stop new sponsored exposure without invalidating pending draws.

Also, `maxOddsBps` is the wrong unit for 1 in 100,000: that probability is **0.1 basis points**. Use an adequately precise fraction or fixed-point representation.

## 9. Changes I would accept

There is plenty worth keeping.

| Recommendation | My position |
|---|---|
| One wrapper supporting ETH and ENGINE modes | **Accept.** It fits the product and avoids two overlapping pack integrations. |
| Public purchases of circulated tokens from hour zero | **Accept.** The original-supply restriction is the meaningful constraint. |
| Fixed ordinary board and like-for-like replacement | **Accept.** It simplifies budgeting and makes the offer more predictable. |
| Transparent mint-fee and platform-fee breakdown | **Accept.** Your mint income is a real player-funded cost. |
| Separate original inventory custody | **Conditional.** Accept only with complete proceeds, liquidity, and routing rules. |
| 5% delayed ETH exit | **Conditional.** Snapshot rights and model actual conversion behavior. |
| Sponsor rate and cap cut to 1×/1× | **Reject as justified.** The expected compensation calculation is incomplete. |
| Full removal of depositor and retirement machinery together | **Split the decision.** Remove depositor rights; retain necessary accounting and retirement. |

These recommendations correspond to the plan's recorded A1–A7 decisions. Several are product changes, not merely implementation details, so I would explicitly revise those entries rather than allowing the code to settle the ambiguity.

On your compensation specifically: **I would keep the existing mint fee.** The review correctly identifies it as the largest individual take in the modeled ticket. That warrants prominent disclosure, not an automatic promise that you will recycle it into maintenance. Your goal includes earning income; any voluntary subsidy should be separate and explicit.

## 10. Change the implementation order

The plan currently puts substantial game surgery before validating the replacement market architecture. That is the wrong order for the main unresolved risk.

I would require this sequence:

1. **Recover existing branches and model artifacts.** Establish what is reusable.
2. **Build a small market proof on a real mainnet fork.** Demonstrate primary purchase, holder sale, public resale purchase, fee capture, and finalization, with full asset conservation.
3. **Rerun the minimal economic scenarios for that exact market**, including zero ENGINE conversion. Do not reuse outputs from the old liquidity architecture.
4. **Then simplify the game and integrate the wrapper, epoch accounting, and treasury.**
5. **Finish sponsor support, interface, and full lifecycle testing.**

That does not require a new broad research effort. It puts the most consequential unproven component ahead of the largest refactor.

A final cleanup: the plan alternates between a **72-hour** timelock and a **30-day** timelock in its deployment phase. Resolve that contradiction. Mainnet-fork integration should remain the primary acceptance environment for existing dependencies; a Sepolia rehearsal is supplementary, not a replacement for testing the real FWA, Shapes, and PoolManager contracts.

## Bottom line

**The planning agent has improved the organization and identified genuine tradeoffs. It has not yet produced an implementation-ready market and accounting design.**

The most important notes to send back are:

**Recover the existing work. Fix the transfer gate. Specify how primary purchases fund selling liquidity. Recalculate the sponsor offer. Preserve operating-capital and shutdown protections. Reconcile epoch and pack lifecycle accounting.**

I would proceed after those revisions. **This is a targeted correction pass, not a reason to reopen the entire product or start another tokenomics exploration.**

### TLDR

**Keep the overall system and most of the simplifications. Do not approve the new market architecture or sponsor terms as settled yet.** The market's primary-sale ETH path is missing, the transfer restriction blocks normal users, and the supposedly fair sponsor offer can return only about 19 ETH in expectation for a 30 ETH Punk under continuous exposure without reclaiming.

Fix those, preserve the game's financial safety checks, and validate the market before undertaking the large game refactor.
